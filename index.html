Get to know me

Hi! I'm Frites, and welcome to my website! I'm 17, Romanian, and I go by any pronouns, though preferrably you'd use he/him. I'm a Virgo, and I am an ENFP.

This is my portfolio. You may be wondering why it's not particularly formal.
Regardless of if you are a potential employer or just a random person, you are still getting to know me by reading this webpage. I find my personality shouldn't be hidden behind a façade of professionalism - I want to be as honest as possible with anyone who checks this out. An important aspect of a person, to me, is sincerity. Therefore, it's only natural that I would want to show you what I'm actually like, instead of wearing a mask.

Why do I develop?

And what do I even make?

Even as a little kid, I always wanted to make stuff. Given the fact I was nerdy and found online games super entertaining, my mind naturally went, "Hey, I could do this game stuff." In particular, there was a man, 'DaniDev.' His work on games like Muck, Karlson, and Bad Game, is what truly inspired me to get into what I do today.

Lo and behold, I opened up Roblox Studio one day, and made slop. I vividly remember my first project. How proud I was of it, showcasing it to all of my friends. It was a crappy copy of DaniDev's Karlson DEMO, but building the game was fun either way.

It didn't take long for me to grow up and find more inspirations. In the peak of 2020, I discovered UNDERTALE. One discovery led to another, and Toby Fox - the developer - is probably my greatest inspiration to this day. I tried many, many times, to replicate his work, even up to this year, before I decided to collect my own ideas into my projects.

The idea of independent development is what makes me want to pursue a career in software development generally. Some of my favourite independent developers aren't even limited to games - consider Linus Torvalds, the creator of Linux, or Richard Stallman, the founder of the Free Software Foundation. Those two are primarily responsible for me ever even getting into IT in the first place, and exploring what it actually means to work in 'software development.'
